# Label noise in short possession spells and event–tracking synchronisation

Context: our "ctrl" labels come from DFL BallPossession (IDSSE/Sportec open data), filtered by own Play/ShotAtGoal events within +-1.5 s. Events are aligned to frames by kick-off offset only. Measured vs speculative is marked throughout.

## 1. How large are event timestamp errors, and what does synchronisation fix?

### Takeaway
Measured raw pass timestamps in manual event data have a median error of about 0.5 s, and only ~14% fall within 0.5 s of the true release frame. The error distribution also has a heavy tail, with means of ~2.3 s and individual cases above 10 s. A constant offset (kick-off alignment) fixes part of this: within-0.5 s rises from ~14% to ~50%. Trajectory-based synchronisation (Biermann 2023, ELASTIC) reaches 70–88% exact or near-exact. Our +-1.5 s touch window therefore covers the typical error but not the tail. Kick-off-only alignment leaves roughly half the events off by more than 0.5 s.

### Cited Findings
- **Biermann, Komitova, Raabe, Müller-Budack, Ewerth, Memmert (2023), "Synchronization of passes in event and spatiotemporal soccer data", Scientific Reports 13:15878** (MEASURED). Data: 4 top-flight European matches from 2014/15, provider not named. Raw provider pass timestamps compared with expert frame-level release labels: mean absolute error 2.34 s, median 0.52 s, Q95 0.84 s; 13.55% within 0.48 s; 25.09% within 0.96 s; 0.59% exact frame. Mean > Q95 implies a few very large outliers. Most provider timestamps come *after* the true release. A per-half constant-offset correction alone raises within-0.48 s to 49.88%. Their best sync: median 0.12 s, 70.34% within 0.48 s, 82.00% within 0.96 s; with outlier removal, 81.22% within 0.48 s. Count mismatch: 2,404 provider passes vs 2,552 expert passes, and only 1,690 were usable after sequence-consistency matching. Code is not stated; data not shareable; article CC BY 4.0. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10518005/)
- **Kim et al. (2025), "ELASTIC: Event-Tracking Data Synchronization in Soccer Without Annotated Event Locations", ECML PKDD 2025 MLSA workshop, arXiv:2508.09238** (MEASURED). Ground truth: 2,134 events from 3 Eredivisie 2024–25 first halves, re-annotated frame by frame by 3 authors at 25 fps. Annotator mean disagreement was 0.96 frames for starts and 0.59 frames for ends. Event-start accuracy for ETSY: exact 21.1%, within 0.2 s 58.3%, within 1 s 74.7%, within 2 s 83.4%; mean error 15.2 frames (~0.6 s). ELASTIC: exact 88.4%, 92.8%, 95.0%, 97.0%; mean 2.3 frames. ELASTIC event *end* (reception) detection: 92.6% exact, mean 1.6 frames; ETSY does not detect ends. Minor events (tackles, fouls, bad touches, take-ons) "often lack clear observable cues": all 3 annotators agreed exactly on only 61.0% of them, vs 90.4% overall. The paper reports Biermann et al.'s method aligning <10% of passes exactly on this data. The paper gives no raw-provider error in seconds. A tutorial uses the Sportec Open (DFL) data, first removing a constant offset. Code: https://github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/elastic (licence not stated on the paper page). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09238v1)
- **Kim, Choi, Lee, Seo, Boomstra, Yoon, Park (2026), "ELASTIC: Trajectory-Based Synchronization of Event and Tracking Data in Soccer", CIKM 2026, arXiv:2608.30227.** This extended version uses candidate-frame extraction plus an extended Needleman–Wunsch alignment. According to a LinkedIn post, it adds a benchmark built by re-annotating 3 Sportec Open Bundesliga matches frame by frame, which would be directly relevant to our data. The full-text numbers could not be extracted (PDF only); paper CC BY 4.0. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30227); benchmark claim via [search snippet/LinkedIn](https://kr.linkedin.com/in/euiseo) (UNVERIFIED)
- **Van Roy, Cascioli, Davis (2023), "ETSY: A rule-based approach to Event and Tracking data SYnchronization"**, KU Leuven, MLSA 2023 / Springer CCIS. Two named error sources are a constant clock offset per period and human annotation error that causes drift; neither is quantified in seconds. Method: estimate the bias from the ball's peak acceleration within 2 m of the kicker in the first 5 s of each period, then search +-5 s windows for open-play actions and +-10 s for set pieces. 313 matches; ETSY synchronised 91.61% of events (≈8% left unmatched), the timestamp approach 99.83%, sync.soccer 100%. No accuracy reported (no ground truth). Code: github.com/ML-KULeuven/ETSY (licence not stated on the blog). — [DTAI blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/etsy:-a-rule-based-approach-to-event-and-tracking-data-synchronization)
- **Anzer & Bauer**: feature-weighted frame matching, originally for shot events. Weights are tuned by grid search on a labelled set, and the method does not correct the constant clock bias. No error figures in seconds were found. — described in [ETSY blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/etsy:-a-rule-based-approach-to-event-and-tracking-data-synchronization) and [Biermann 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10518005/)
- **Vidal-Codina, Evans, El Fakir, Billingham (2022), "Automatic event detection in football using tracking data", Sports Engineering, arXiv:2202.00804** (MIT/FIFA). States that annotated event times can be **more than 10 s** before or after the actual event (benchmark events from Sportec Solutions, i.e. the DFL provider). Also states that manual tagging differs by 10–15% between analysts. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804)
- **Liu, Hopkins, Gómez, Molinuevo (2013), "Inter-operator reliability of live football match statistics from OPTA Sportsdata", IJPAS 13(3):803–821** (MEASURED, 1 La Liga match, 2 trained operator groups). Team-event kappa 0.92 and 0.94; mean timing difference between operators ~0.06 s. This measures operator-to-operator consistency, not error relative to the tracking frame. — [VU repository](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/37042/)
- **Sportec/IDSSE open data (Bassek et al. 2025, Scientific Data, CC BY 4.0)**: 7 matches (Bundesliga 1 and 2). Event and tracking streams need a constant offset removed before frame assignment, as done in ELASTIC and other tools. — [HF README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pysport/idsse-data/blob/main/README.md); [databallpy sync docs](https://databallpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started/synchronisation_page.html)

### Inferences
- Kick-off-only alignment is roughly the "constant offset" baseline (~50% of passes within 0.5 s in Biermann's data). A +-1.5 s touch window probably captures most events, since raw Q95 was 0.84 s in Biermann. The tail of 2–10 s errors (Vidal-Codina; mean 2.34 s) will both remove real short spells and keep spurious ones. This holds only if DFL data behaves like Biermann's unnamed provider, which is unverified.
- Because provider timestamps tend to lag the true release (Biermann), a spell that starts or ends at an event-derived time may be shifted by ~0.5 s. For a spell under 2 s, that is 25%+ of its length. Boundary error therefore matters proportionally more for short spells. This is arithmetic, not a measured result on our data.

## 2. How do PathCRF, Vidal-Codina and Biermann build possession labels, and how many touches are missing from raw events?

### Takeaway
State-of-the-art pipelines on the same Sportec data (PathCRF) do not use the DFL BallPossession field. They (1) synchronise events with ELASTIC, (2) detect extra touches from ball-trajectory direction changes (RDP), (3) align them with Needleman–Wunsch, (4) insert unmatched touches, and (5) derive possession from the refined event sequence. They report that raw events miss touches but give no percentage. Rule-based possession from tracking (Vidal-Codina) relies on possession zones plus angle/speed-change criteria, and rejects "control" without a ball deflection.

### Cited Findings
- **Kim et al. (2026), "PathCRF: Ball-Free Soccer Event Detection via Possession Path Inference from Player Trajectories", arXiv:2602.12080** (KAIST). Data: Sportec Open DFL, 7 matches (5/1/1 split, also leave-one-match-out); 25→5 fps. Labels: events synchronised with ELASTIC because manual timestamps are imprecise. Raw events "miss some ball touches", so RDP detects abrupt ball-direction changes as candidate touches. These are aligned to the events by Needleman–Wunsch, and unmatched touches are inserted as extra ball-touch events. The refined events become per-step possession edges (self-loop = control/dribble, sender→receiver = ball in flight). Only in-play segments are used. Events count as detected within **1 s** tolerance, same type and player; relaxed recall is ~80% at 1 s/4 m and 86.1% at 2 s/10 m. Model results: 69.64% edge accuracy, 75.69% event F1, 92.40% frame-level team-possession accuracy. No count of inserted touches, no RDP parameters, no duration distribution reported. Code: github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/pathcrf. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12080)
- **Vidal-Codina et al. (2022)**. Possession zone is 0.5 m (Track160) or 1 m (Tracab, Hawk-Eye); a 1 m duel zone takes precedence. A gain requires a change in ball direction or speed, and control sequences without either are discarded as false positives. Ball is smoothed with a Savitzky–Golay filter over 7 frames. A player who loses and regains the ball with no other interaction keeps possession. Hyperparameters are in the supplement. Pass precision and recall are >90%; shots are 78% precision, 53% recall. Manual data had 254 shot misclassifications and 569 crosses tagged as passes. About 15% of shots vs <6% of passes were missed or misattributed. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804)
- **databallpy** implements Vidal-Codina-style player possession (default 1.5 m zone, angle change >10°, speed change threshold; `min_frames_pz` default 0, so no minimum duration). It also implements an event-based team possession: switch on the first successful opposing event, no hysteresis. On the open DFL game, event-based team possession agrees with the provider's possession field on ~89.9% of alive frames (5,298/5,892; our calculation from the notebook counts). One divergence window lasts ~10 s. The notebook says the provider's definition is undocumented, may credit possession after an unsuccessful pass, and "is not always clear, especially in ground and aerial duels". — [databallpy docs](https://databallpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/team_and_player_possession.html)
- **Biermann et al. (2021), "A Unified Taxonomy and Multimodal Dataset for Events in Invasion Games", MMSports '21, arXiv:2108.11149**. Defines possession and events minimally and objectively. See Q3 for agreement numbers. Dataset: github.com/mm4spa/eigd (licence not stated in the paper). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11149)
- **"Ball Radar"**: no source found under that name. The closest hit is Witt, Völker & Eskofier (2016), detection of single ball contacts with radio tracking (ball 2000 Hz). — [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2016/60811)

### Inferences
- Our ctrl filter assumes that a team with no Play/Shot event in +-1.5 s had no real touch. PathCRF's pipeline exists precisely because raw events *miss* touches. Some ctrl removals may therefore delete real short spells, such as deflections, first touches and failed controls, and some kept spells may be shifted. Both effects are plausible; neither is quantified.
- Our 6% vs 30% (<2 s) gap between ctrl and raw BallPossession is consistent with the provider field flickering in duels, given the databallpy remarks on duels and the 10% frame disagreement. This is not proof.

### Gaps
- No published percentage of touches missing from raw DFL events. **Next step:** run RDP + Needleman–Wunsch (PathCRF/ELASTIC code) on IDSSE and count inserted touches and how many fall inside ctrl-removed spells.

## 3. How accurate are provider possession fields, and how well do humans agree on possession?

### Takeaway
Humans do not agree well on possession boundaries. Temporal IoU for ball possession between expert annotators is only 0.72–0.78, while event-level agreement on passes and receptions is 92–96%. A provider's real-time pass labels matched experts in only ~13% of cases at a tight tolerance. No public specification of DFL BallPossession lag or hysteresis was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Biermann et al. 2021**. Inter-annotator temporal IoU: ball possession 0.72 (handball) and 0.78 (soccer); game status 0.68 and 0.92. Inexperienced vs experienced annotators on soccer possession: 0.73. Event-level agreement (nearest-neighbour P/R): reception 92.2–94.9%, successful pass 92.4–96.0%. Disagreement grows with event complexity. An anonymised commercial provider (PDD-S, 2014/15) vs gold standard gave successful pass P/R 12.9%/12.6%, shot 6.8%/7.1%, game status change 74.7%/73.5%. The authors attribute this to real-time manual annotation. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11149)
- **ELASTIC 2025**: even careful frame-level annotators agree exactly on only 61.0% of minor events (bad touches, tackles, take-ons). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09238v1)
- **Vidal-Codina 2022**: 10–15% tagging differences among analysts; errors in timestamps and players in manual data mean 100% detection is unachievable. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804)
- **databallpy**: ~10% frame disagreement between event-derived and provider team possession on the open DFL match; provider definition undocumented. — [databallpy](https://databallpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/team_and_player_possession.html)
- **floodlight** parses DFL BallPossession and BallStatus codes per frame but does not document their semantics. — [floodlight DFL io](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/latest/modules/io/dfl.html)
- **Liu et al. 2013 (Opta)**: high operator agreement (kappa 0.92–0.94), but this was trained operators on a single match. — [VU](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/37042/)

### Inferences
- A possession IoU of ~0.75 between experts implies that segment-boundary ambiguity is of order ~1 s per spell. This is our rough reading: for a 4 s spell, an IoU of 0.75 corresponds to ~1 s of disagreement. Spells under 2 s are therefore near the human ambiguity floor, and a recall of ~21% on <2 s ctrl spells may be partly capped by label ambiguity. This needs testing on our data.

### Gaps
- No official DFL/Sportec definition of BallPossession: no lag, hysteresis or rule for who is credited during duels was found. Bialkowski-specific label-noise work was not found in this pass.

## 4. How do others learn with noisy boundaries and short segments?

### Takeaway
Temporal action segmentation offers boundary-tolerant tools: temporal label smoothing at boundaries, adaptive boundary smoothing, EM/random-walk losses that leave boundary regions uncertain, and boundary-aware pooling. Robustness tests show that shifting boundaries by up to +-25 frames degrades performance only modestly. The sports pipelines handle this mainly through evaluation tolerance (1–2 s) instead of special losses.

### Cited Findings
- **DIR-AS, arXiv:2304.02110**: Temporal Label Smoothing relabels only boundary frames towards the adjacent classes, because the classifier is unreliable at ambiguous boundaries. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02110)
- **Ding & Yao, Adaptive Boundary Smoothing (ECCV 2022, arXiv:2207.08653)**: builds coarser boundaries for more robust learning in semi-supervised segmentation (5–50% labels). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08653v2)
- **Hirsch et al., Random Walks for Temporal Action Segmentation with Timestamp Supervision (WACV 2024)**: identifies uncertainty regions around boundaries. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/html/Hirsch_Random_Walks_for_Temporal_Action_Segmentation_With_Timestamp_Supervision_WACV_2024_paper.html)
- **ECCV 2022 timestamp-supervision EM loss**: probabilities become uncertain mainly near boundaries; it beats cross-entropy on 50Salads and Breakfast. — [ECCV supp](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136640276-supp.pdf)
- **Boundary-Aware Cascade Networks (ECCV 2020)**: local barrier pooling reduces over-segmentation. — [ECVA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/4845_ECCV_2020_paper.php)
- **Spectral Scalpel (arXiv:2603.24134, 2026)**: training with boundaries jittered by up to +-25 frames lowered performance modestly. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24134)
- **PathCRF and ELASTIC** evaluate with 1 s (and 2 s) tolerance windows. — [PathCRF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12080); [ELASTIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09238v1)

### Inferences
- Applicable to us: (a) score short-spell recall with a +-0.5–1 s boundary tolerance, as PathCRF and ELASTIC do; (b) down-weight or smooth labels within ~0.5 s of ctrl boundaries; (c) re-derive labels with ELASTIC sync plus RDP touch insertion and compare short-spell recall across label versions. That comparison would directly measure how much of the 21% recall is label artefact. These are proposed steps, not results.

### Gaps
- No source found that directly quantifies how short noisy segments cap recall in sports possession models. Co-teaching applied to sports labels was not found in this pass.
