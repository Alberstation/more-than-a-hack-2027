# How providers and standards define frame-level possession and control (emphasis on short possessions)

Note on method: about 20 tool calls. Primary sources were read where they were reachable: the Bassek et al. 2025 full text on PMC, kloppy source code, Link & Hoernig 2017, Vidal-Codina 2022 (ar5iv), PathCRF 2026, FIFA EFI v1.1, the Wyscout glossary, databallpy docs and Yamamoto et al. 2023. Some pages could not be reached: the DFL Catalogue of Definitions is not public, the Stats Perform possessions page returned 403, and the SkillCorner PDF specs were not read. Each item below is labelled as documented or inferred.

## 1. How DFL / Sportec generate BallPossession and BallStatus in the position data

### Takeaway
The only public documentation is one sentence in Bassek et al. 2025. It says BallPossession is "defined by the player in control of the ball" (1 = home, 2 = away), and BallStatus is 0/1 for whether the referee has interrupted play. No public source documents the algorithm, whether operators set the field, the minimum duration, or whether touches are included. The field definitions point to the DFL "Catalogue of Definitions Official Match Data v5.1" (2020), which is not publicly available.

### Cited Findings
- Bassek, M., Rein, R., Weber, H., Memmert, D. (2025). "An integrated dataset of spatiotemporal and event data in elite soccer." Scientific Data 12. DOI 10.1038/s41597-025-04505-y. CC-BY 4.0 with DFL authorization. 7 matches: 2 from Bundesliga and 5 from 2. Bundesliga, 2022/23. 1,002,644 frames, 11,137 events, 207 players — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented, quoted: "the ball possession status (BallPossession; 1 = home team in possession, 2 = away team in possession)"; "the match state (BallStatus; 0 = ball inactive, 1 = ball active)"; "The ball possession is defined by the player in control of the ball."; "The match state is inactive when the match is interrupted by the referee" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented: the position data come from TRACAB Gen5 (ChyronHego), using 16–20 cameras at 1920×1080 and 25 Hz. Post-processing filters outliers and smooths the data. Player identities are assigned manually before the match. Identity swaps "are resolved by the live operators or during the final quality control". "all data are visually controlled by live operators for plausibility", and there is a final quality check after each game — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented: event data are collected by a pool of 120 trained operators, with 5 per match (a speaker and a writer for the live stream, plus 2 who add data and check quality). A domain-expert supervisor then controls quality. "All data categories are defined in the Catalogue of Definitions published by the DFL" (v5.1, 2020) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented: event-to-tracking synchronisation used DataBallPy v0.5.3 with Needleman-Wunsch on 6,251 passes and shots. The mean shift was about −0.37 s, and the residual player–ball distance was 2.61 ± 3.60 m (max 75.35 m) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented: the paper says nothing about possession duration, touches, or how BallPossession is computed. "Ball control" appears only as an xG feature and as the event attribute BallPossessionPhase — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- Documented: the GitHub README (spoho-datascience/idsse-data) only says "Official position data captured by TRACAB" and gives the CC-BY 4.0 licence. It contains no field semantics — [GitHub](https://github.com/spoho-datascience/idsse-data)
- Documented (code): kloppy's Sportec deserializer maps `BallStatus == 1` to ALIVE and everything else to DEAD. It maps `BallPossession == 1` to home and **any other value** to away, so it never tests for 2 and has no "no possession" state. There are no comments on how DFL generates the field — [kloppy sportec deserializer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PySport/kloppy/master/kloppy/infra/serializers/tracking/sportec/deserializer.py)
- Documented (empirical, on Sportec-type data): the databallpy notebook compared its event-based team possession with the provider's possession. In the sample game it found about 320 alive frames labelled home by events but away by the provider, and 274 the other way round, with stretches as long as about 10 s (frames 859–1005). In the example shown, the provider counted the frames after an unsuccessful away pass as away possession — [databallpy docs](https://databallpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/team_and_player_possession.html)
- Documented: the DFL Catalogue of Definitions ("Definitionskatalog Offizielle Spieldaten") is not publicly downloadable. Forum users ask for it — [BigSoccer thread](https://www.bigsoccer.com/threads/does-anyone-have-the-definitionskatalog-offizielle-spieldaten.2080544/)

### Inferences
- Inferred: the field is binary home/away with no "contested/none" value. In every live frame it must name one team, so through flights, duels and loose balls it most likely carries over the last controlling team (hold-last behaviour). This fits the databallpy observation that possession stays with a team after its failed pass. The frame-level ground truth therefore likely switches only when the other team gains control, and a mere touch by the opponent should not flip it. This matches the challenge's written "controlled possession" definition, but it is not confirmed for DFL.
- Inferred: no source says whether BallPossession is set live by operators, derived from event data such as BallPossessionPhase, or derived algorithmically from tracking. "Defined by the player in control" and the operator-driven QC suggest a human-in-the-loop or event-derived signal, not a pure distance rule. That matters for short possessions: the timing of a switch could lag or lead the physical touch by a few frames, in the same way the event timestamps carry about 0.37 s of offset.
- Inferred: nothing documented sets a minimum duration, so a short switch (under 1 s) is possible in principle. Its frequency in the 7 matches should be measured directly from the data rather than assumed.

### Gaps
- The DFL Catalogue of Definitions v5.1 text for "Ballbesitz/BallPossession" in position data was not found publicly.
- No Sportec Solutions technical documentation on how the BallPossession field is generated was found.
- The floodlight docs were not checked for field comments; the Bassek paper only cites floodlight v0.4.0 as a loader.

## 2. How other providers and standards define possession, control, touch and sequence, and how they treat very short possessions

### Takeaway
Event providers (Opta, Wyscout, StatsBomb) define team possession by sequences of events. A possession changes only when the opponent gains control: lost duels, rebounds and deflections recovered by the same team do not end it. FIFA EFI is the main standard with an explicit third state, "In Contest" (loose ball after aerial duels, blocks and similar contacts). So "touch without control does not change possession" is the industry norm. Where a contested state exists, it removes short contested phases from both teams' possession.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA Enhanced Football Intelligence (EFI) Explanation Document v1.1 (FIFA, 11 July 2023). Possession Control has four states: Team A, Team B, "In Contest" ("in play but with neither team in controlled possession"), and Out of Play. "In Contest" is "triggered by certain events": for example, an aerial duel where "contact on the ball by a player during the contest will initiate a loose ball state", or a defender's block, where the contact puts the ball in a loose state. The states "are calculated based on the sequence of events performed by the players". No time thresholds are given — [FIFA EFI v1.1 PDF](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/tournaments/womens-world-cup/2023/FIFA%20Enhanced%20Football%20Intelligence%20(EFI)%20Explainations_EN%20v1.1.pdf)
- FIFA EFI, Ball Recovery Time: "the time difference between the last ball control event of a team in its possession sequence and the first ball control event on the following possession sequence. In between these possession sequences there can be possession sequence by the opponent team and/or a ball in contest state." — [FIFA EFI v1.1 PDF](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/tournaments/womens-world-cup/2023/FIFA%20Enhanced%20Football%20Intelligence%20(EFI)%20Explainations_EN%20v1.1.pdf)
- Wyscout glossary: a possession is "a sequence of events with the ball of the same team". It is measured by time, and out-of-play time counts for neither team. Lost duels, missed balls and rebounds the same team immediately recovers do not end it. Open-play possession duration classes: short 0–10 s, medium 10–20 s, long 20–45 s, very long >45 s — [Wyscout glossary](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/ball_possession/)
- Opta/Stats Perform (secondary summary from search results; the primary page returned 403): a touch is credited even when the ball bounces off a player without intent, and a miscontrol is recorded as an unsuccessful touch. A possession ends when the opposing side gains control. A "sequence" is a passage of play belonging to one team that ends with a defensive action, a stoppage or a shot — [Stats Perform sequences and possessions](https://www.statsperform.com/page-block-preview/advanced-metrics-sequences-and-possessions/), [Opta event definitions](https://optaplayerstats.statsperform.com/de_DE/opta-event-definitions), [The Analyst definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions). Not verified verbatim.
- StatsBomb open data: each event has a `possession` index ("the current unique possession in the game") and a `possession_team` ("The ID of the team that started this possession in control of the ball"). Counterpress is defined as pressing actions within 5 s of an open-play turnover — [socceraction StatsBomb schema](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/generated/socceraction.data.statsbomb.StatsBombEventSchema.html), [StatsBombR possessioninfo.R](https://rdrr.io/github/statsbomb/StatsBombR/src/R/possessioninfo.R)
- SkillCorner open data: tracking at 10 fps. `possession` is a dict with `player_id` and `group` ("which player/team (home or away team) are in possession"); older versions use `trackable_object` + `group`. Dynamic events include `player_possession` rows carrying EPV and pressure labels. The README does not give derivation rules or minimum durations and refers to external PDF specs. MIT licence. The current sample is 10 A-League 2024/25 matches — [SkillCorner opendata GitHub](https://github.com/SkillCorner/opendata), [tracking tutorial](https://skillcorner.github.io/opendata/notebooks/tutorials/03_Basics_of_Tracking/Open_Data_Tracking_Tutorial/)
- floodlight's SkillCorner loader maps "home team" to 1 and "away team" to 2 — [floodlight skillcorner source](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/floodlight/io/skillcorner.html)

### Inferences
- Inferred: the challenge's written rule (a simple touch, a non-intercepting deflection, or an aerial duel won without control does not change possession) matches Opta, Wyscout and FIFA EFI usage. FIFA would label those moments "In Contest". A binary provider field like DFL's has no such state and would most likely keep the previous team.
- Inferred: under these definitions, short possessions (<2 s) are mostly one-touch passes, first-time clearances that reach a teammate, and quick interceptions followed by an immediate loss. Contested touches would mostly not register as possessions.

### Gaps
- Second Spectrum, Hawk-Eye, Tracab/ChyronHego and Metrica possession definitions were not found in the accessible sources, and the remaining budget did not allow a search.
- No public FIFA EPTS or FIFA Football Language definition text was retrieved. EFI v1.1 is the FIFA source used here.
- The verbatim Opta definitions of "touch", "possession" and "sequence" were not retrieved (403).
- The SkillCorner PDF specs (thresholds for `player_possession`) were not read.

## 3. Academic possession definitions from tracking: thresholds and short-possession behaviour

### Takeaway
Rule-based methods combine three ingredients: a distance zone (0.5–1.5 m), a kick or control trigger (acceleration ≥4 m/s², or a change in ball speed or direction), and an end rule with a look-ahead (≈1 s). They count a touch as possession only if it changes the ball's motion. They separate "possession" from "control": Link & Hoernig credit possession without control in ping-pong and header situations. Vidal-Codina treats duels as a separate state that overrides possession. PathCRF, the only one using the same 7 Sportec matches, reaches 92.40% frame-level team possession accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Link, D. & Hoernig, M. (2017). "Individual ball possession in soccer." PLOS ONE 12(7): e0179953. They distinguish Individual Ball Possession (IBP, from the moment a player can act on the ball until another player's IBP), Individual Ball Action (IBA), Individual Ball Control (IBC, a subset of IBA when the player "can choose among several play options"), Team Ball Possession, Team Ball Control, and Team Playmaking — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0179953)
  - Data and thresholds: 25 Hz, RTS smoothing, ball-jump sections under 1 s excluded. Start uses a distance threshold T_P plus nearest player. Kick detection requires a local ball acceleration maximum ≥ 4 m/s². End uses ball prediction over a 1 s horizon, and the IBA ends if the predicted distance stays ≥ T_A for 1 s. The values of T_P, T_A and T_K are trained but not reported. Control is classified with a Bayesian network (K2) on IBA duration, ball speed/acceleration, player–ball distance and opponents within 0.5–5 m. Evaluation tolerance is 0.6 s.
  - Short contacts: no touch is required. A touch below 4 m/s² may be missed. "Ping-pong" midfield sequences and headers after crosses count as possession but not as IBC. A brief loss of control with no opposing IBA stays within one IBP. No minimum duration is reported.
  - Numbers: 60 Bundesliga matches (2012/13 or 2013/14; the paper is inconsistent). 69,667 IBAs, of which 53,354 are IBC, so about 1,160 IBAs per match. IBC intervals 836 ± 424 per match. Team Ball Possession 56:04 ± 5:12 min, Team Ball Control 17:49 ± 8:13 min per match. Central-forward mean contact time 0.9 ± 0.6 s. Validation on 5 manually annotated matches (6,976 IBAs): F = .88 for IBA and .83 for IBP. Control kappa .67 against an inter-rater kappa of .72. 97.0% of control intervals and 50% of non-control intervals were classified correctly. No code was released.
- Vidal-Codina, F., Evans, N., El Fakir, B., Billingham, J. (2022). "Automatic event detection in football using tracking data." Sports Engineering 25. DOI 10.1007/s12283-022-00381-6, arXiv 2202.00804 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804), [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/145347)
  - Possession zone radius R_pz = 0.5 m (Track160) or 1 m (Tracab, Hawk-Eye). Duel zone R_dz = 1 m around the ball, with a duel when ≥2 opponents are inside, and "the duel condition prevails" over possession. Ball x–y smoothed with Savitzky-Golay (order 2, 7 frames ≈ 0.25 s at 25 Hz). 2D only, with no z.
  - A gain requires the ball to be inside the PZ plus a direction change (dot product < ε_θ) or a speed change > ε_v. Otherwise the frames are discarded as false positives, so a ball passing a player does not count. A loss requires the ball to be outside the PZ at f+1 with displacement > ε_s, and the player absent from the next control frame. A brief loss regained by the same player is not a loss. For goalkeepers, a loss within 1 s separates a deflection from retention. The ε values are only in the supplementary material, which was not read.
  - Data: 25 Hz from Track160, Tracab 4.0/5.0 (including 15 Bundesliga 2019/20 games) and Hawk-Eye. Passes reach >90% precision and recall (>30K passes). Shots reach P 78 / R 53, with 70% of the 254 shot errors being blocked shots labelled as passes. Runtime is about 3 min per match. Code is not stated.
- databallpy (Python, open source) implements Vidal-Codina for individual possession with defaults `pz_radius` = 1.5 m, `bv_threshold` = 5.0 m/s, `ba_threshold` = 10°, `min_frames_pz` = 0 (no short-touch filter). Duels between opponents are left "out of scope". Possession ends at the last frame inside the PZ unless the player is carrying the ball — [databallpy docs](https://databallpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/team_and_player_possession.html), [GitHub alek050/databallpy](https://github.com/alek050/databallpy)
- Kim, H. et al. (2026). "PathCRF: Ball-Free Soccer Event Detection via Possession Path Inference from Player Trajectories." arXiv 2602.12080, CC BY 4.0 (paper). Code at github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/pathcrf (licence not stated) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12080)
  - Same 7 Sportec/DFL matches (5 train, 1 val, 1 test), no ball input. Ground truth is built from events synced with ELASTIC. Because raw events miss some touches, extra touch points come from abrupt ball-direction changes (Ramer–Douglas–Peucker) aligned by Needleman–Wunsch. Each frame is labelled with a single edge: self-loop = control, (u,v) = ball in transit, or an out-of-pitch node. Windows are 10 s at 5 FPS, upsampled to 25 FPS.
  - Results: frame-level team possession accuracy 92.40%. Home possession 62.13% ground truth vs 62.64% predicted. Edge accuracy 69.64%. Event F1 75.69% (P 73.18 / R 78.37) with a 1 s tolerance. Test match has 1,831 ground-truth events. The paper does not use the BallPossession field as its label and reports no duration breakdown.
- Morra/Borghesi TCN and Peral/TranSPORTmer: not retrieved. The PathCRF summary says TranSPORTmer uses a vanilla set-attention block, and that the Ball Radar PPE+FPE social module performed better in its ablation (secondary review, unverified) — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/pathcrf-ball-free-soccer-event-detection-via-possession-path-inference-from-player-trajectories)

### Inferences
- Inferred: the academic consensus treats an "on-ball moment" as possession only when ball kinematics change (acceleration or speed/direction thresholds). Duels or 2+ opponents near the ball form a separate state. A model aimed at a "controlled possession" target should therefore benefit from features such as ball acceleration and direction-change peaks, the count of opponents within about 1 m, and a ≈1 s look-ahead for loss confirmation. These are the ingredients every rule-based method uses.
- Inferred: PathCRF's labels are built from events plus touch detection, not from BallPossession. Its 92.40% team-possession accuracy is the closest public reference on the same 7 matches, but it is not directly comparable with a BallPossession-derived ground truth.

### Gaps
- Numeric values of ε_s, ε_θ, ε_v (Vidal-Codina supplement) and T_P, T_A, T_K (Link & Hoernig) are not reported in the main texts.
- No paper reports accuracy stratified by possession duration (<2 s vs longer).
- Ball Radar (Kim et al.), Morra/Borghesi TCN and TranSPORTmer (Peral et al.) thresholds were not retrieved in this budget.

## 4. Statistics on the distribution of possession durations

### Takeaway
Player-level possessions are short: a mean of 2.49 s in J1 League tracking plus play-by-play data, fitted by a gamma distribution with shape 2.29 and scale 1.09 s. That implies a large share under 2 s. At team level, 11.3% of team possessions last under 1 s. No source gives a frame-level share for possessions under 1–2 s under a binary provider field.

### Cited Findings
- Yamamoto, K., Uezu, T., Kagawa, K., Yamazaki, Y., Narizuka, T. (2023). "Theory and data analysis of player and team ball possession time in football." arXiv 2308.05460. Data: 95 J1 League matches (2020–21), tracking every 0.04 s plus play-by-play touches — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.05460)
  - Player possession runs from a touch until another player touches the ball or play stops. Mean 2.49 s for outfield players and 4.61 s for goalkeepers. Gamma fit β = 2.29, τ = 1.09 s. About 1,225 player possessions per match (the text rendering is garbled).
  - Team possession: mean 7.24 s, 421.1 per match (≈210 per team), 2.91 players per team possession on average. Possessions under 1 s are 11.3% of team possessions (removed in an inverse-Gaussian trial fit, with μ = 8.10 s and λ = 5.76 s).
- Link & Hoernig (2017): central-forward mean contact time 0.9 ± 0.6 s, and ≈1,160 individual ball actions per Bundesliga match — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0179953)
- Wyscout's shortest possession class is 0–10 s, which suggests that sub-2 s possessions are not a separate reporting category for event providers — [Wyscout glossary](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/ball_possession/)
- Chacoma et al. (2020, arXiv 2005.04020) study the distribution of possession intervals in Europe's top-5 leagues. Numbers not retrieved — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04020v1)

### Inferences
- Inferred (computed from the published gamma parameters, not reported by the paper): the CDF of Gamma(k = 2.29, θ = 1.09 s) puts about 17% of individual player possessions under 1 s, about 46% under 2 s and about 84% under 4 s (numerical CDF of the published fit). Short individual possessions are therefore the majority case, not an edge case.
- Inferred: team possessions are what an H/A/D target sees, because a teammate pass does not switch the team. They average about 7 s, with 11.3% under 1 s in J1. Short team possessions (<2 s) are probably on the order of 15–25% of possessions by count, but a small share of frames. This should be measured directly on the 7 IDSSE matches.

### Gaps
- No published frame-level share of short possessions under a provider binary field (DFL BallPossession) was found.
- No Bundesliga-specific duration distribution of team possessions was found.
