\section{Approach}

\textbf{The problem as we saw it.} The challenge tracking is automatic and incomplete: the ball is
missing from 41\,\% of the graded frames, players come and go as tracklets, and there are no
identities. No label for the match may be used. So the model has to learn possession on
\emph{other} matches, and those matches have to be made to look like this tracker before learning
from them.

\textbf{Pipeline (v6).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Training data: degraded public matches from two providers.} Seven Bundesliga matches
  from the IDSSE/DFL open dataset and the three Metrica Sports sample games. We degrade them to match
  the challenge tracker. The ball is hidden in gaps drawn from the length distribution of the
  challenge's own gaps, under two patterns: uniformly at random, or concentrated in dead-ball
  stretches as the challenge's seem to be. We also drop player slots for 1--30\,s stretches
  (7\,\% of slot time), add slowly drifting position error (0.25\,m), and make the dead ball
  \emph{move} (the providers freeze or drop it, the challenge tracker does not). Each match is
  degraded three times under both patterns.
\item \textbf{Labels for controlled possession} (\code{control.py}, \S\ref{sec:rules}): the
  providers' possession, corrected so that a team that never plays the ball does not get possession.
\item \textbf{Two per-frame models, mixed.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{gradient-boosted tree model} (scikit-learn \code{HistGradientBoostingClassifier})
    on 85 identity-free features: distances of each team to the ball, who is nearest and when that
    changes, ball kicks, team shape (quantiles, spread, advance), pass chains, retention. Fitted on
    IDSSE. Weight 0.25.
  \item A \textbf{set encoder}: per team, a shared MLP over players (position, velocity, offset to
    the ball) pooled by mean, max and closeness to the ball, followed by a dilated temporal
    convolution seeing about $\pm$50\,s at 5 steps/s; 451\,k parameters, 3 seeds averaged. Fitted
    on IDSSE and Metrica. Weight 0.75. Trained in PyTorch on GPU; at inference it runs in NumPy
    float64, so the submission needs no GPU and no torch.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Decoding.} One three-state Viterbi pass per half over the mixed log-probabilities,
  with a cost of 14 for each change of possession and 20 for each change to or from dead ball.
  This turns flickering per-frame decisions into possessions, which is what \code{spellF1} counts.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why this rather than something else.} Rules on the nearest player fragment possession
wherever the ball is missing (the starter baseline). A model trained on clean tracking learns
signals that the challenge does not have: a frozen dead ball, a ball that is always visible. The
gains came from closing that gap: matching the gap pattern (+0.05 to +0.08 spellF1 on held-out
matches), a moving dead ball (+0.03 to +0.08), labels that follow the definition of controlled
possession, the set encoder's long temporal context (+0.04 to +0.07), and a second provider in the
encoder's training (+0.01 to +0.02). Every constant (decoder costs, mixture weight) is chosen by
leave-one-match-out validation on external data, never on the challenge.

\textbf{Methods tried and dropped}, all measured on held-out external matches, accepted only with
+0.03 paired, at least 5 of 7 matches up, and no loss on Metrica (v6 was accepted below that
threshold because it improved on all four criteria: both providers, both gap patterns):
\begin{itemize}
\item change-point detector feeding the decoder;
\item minimum Bayes risk decoding;
\item re-scoring segments by mean probability, and a two-stage HGB on windowed probabilities;
\item geometry features: Voronoi, pressure, projected arrival;
\item per-possession loss weights and learned transitions in the encoder, to recover short
  possessions;
\item explicit touch evidence on a ball blurred like the challenge's: +0.011 for the HGB alone,
  nothing once mixed with the encoder;
\item Metrica in the HGB's training: no consistent gain on unseen IDSSE matches
  ($-0.005$ / $+0.009$);
\item a minimum length for dead-ball stretches inside the Viterbi decoder: no effect, because the
  decoder costs already rule out stoppages shorter than 2\,s;
\item ball gaps that start where the challenge loses the ball (slow balls, near the lines): not
  needed, the models score the same or better under them (Metrica 0.715 against 0.709);
\item LightGBM, more seeds;
\item in a short screen (one seed, 1000 iterations, 4 folds, paired): a per-player possessor head
  and a ball-state head as auxiliary losses, consistency between clean and degraded views,
  randomised degradation, weight averaging (SWA), mirrored pitches and a delayed team-reaction
  input. None beat the reference by the screening rule;
\item decoder variants: logit mixing, cheaper switches inside ball gaps, a long-possession prior;
\item SkillCorner's 20 open matches (MIT, automatic broadcast tracking) in the encoder's training:
  +0.029 / +0.027 on each held-out Metrica game, but $-0.008$ / $-0.010$ on IDSSE (1--2 of 7 up),
  3 seeds, constants nested. Not submitted: it trades a loss on one provider for a gain on the
  other.
\end{itemize}
Learned CRF models from the literature (PathCRF) need player identities and goalkeepers, which the
challenge does not provide.

\section{Possession rules implemented}\label{sec:rules}

The challenge defines possession as \textbf{controlled}. A contested or loose ball stays with the
last team, and a touch, deflection or aerial duel without control does not change possession. The
DFL \code{BallPossession} field changes on every touch (30\,\% of its possessions last less than
2\,s), so we relabel it (\code{possession/control.py}; Metrica's event-built possession goes
through the same rule):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gain of control.} A spell of a team that plays the ball neither in a pass/play event
  nor in a shot within 1.5\,s of it is a touch, a deflection or a won duel without control. It is
  handed back to the team before it. The 1.5\,s window was fixed before any score was seen and
  never tuned.
\item \textbf{Loose and contested balls} stay with the last controlling team. This follows from the
  rule above, and the decoder cost enforces it: changing possession needs sustained evidence.
\item \textbf{Dead ball (\code{D})}: from the provider's ball status, unchanged. A restart
  (throw-in, corner, free kick, goal kick, kick-off) ends \code{D} when the ball is played. Each
  half is \code{D} until its kick-off.
\item \textbf{Not graded (\code{X})}: everything outside the two halves of the video (before the
  match, halftime, after the final whistle).
\item \textbf{Decoder.} A possession must outweigh a cost of 14 to appear, so very brief spells
  with weak evidence are merged into their neighbours.
\end{itemize}
An audit of these labels on clean tracking found that the short controlled possessions start with a
ball touch by their own team as often as long ones (87--90\,\%), and survive shifts of the event
clock of $\pm$0.5\,s (95--97\,\%). They are real possessions, not alignment artefacts.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Short possessions are missed.} Recall of possessions under 2\,s is about 20\,\%
  (2--4\,s: about 50\,\%; over 16\,s: 91\,\%). In those spells the model gives the true team about
  0.45: the opponent is just as close, and tracking alone does not say who touched the ball.
  Neither reweighting the loss, learned transitions, nor explicit touch features moved this, so
  the model reports fewer, longer possessions than the training labels have.
\item \textbf{Training labels are an approximation.} The challenge ground truth comes from
  corrected tracking by its own rules; ours come from the providers' events through the rule in
  \S\ref{sec:rules}. Where the definitions differ, we inherit the difference.
\item \textbf{Simulated tracker.} The degradation reproduces the gap pattern, the moving dead ball
  and the ball's kinematics (velocity-change quantiles within 10\,\% after a 0.24\,s blur, which
  the models tolerate: v5 scores 0.764 / 0.784 on the blurred ball against 0.767 / 0.779 on the
  sharp one). Gaps placed where an automatic tracker
  tends to lose the ball (slow balls, near the lines) do not hurt the models. Our simulated dead
  ball moves more calmly than the challenge's.
\item \textbf{Two providers for training.} Across providers we lose about 0.1 (IDSSE $\to$
  Metrica); training the encoder on both recovers 0.01--0.02.
\item \textbf{With more time:} labelled matches from an automatic, broadcast-style tracker (the
  closest match to this domain), a ball detector trained on external wide-angle tactical footage to
  fill gaps, and per-player touch attribution from video for the sub-2-second possessions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproduction}

Python 3.12, CPU, pinned dependencies (\code{requirements-solution.txt}). The match data must be in
\code{data/} as distributed; nothing from it is in the repository.

\begin{verbatim}
pip install -r requirements-solution.txt
python possession/idsse.py      # downloads the 7 IDSSE matches and their events
python possession/v5.py         # fits the HGB on degraded IDSSE (shared by v6)
python possession/pipeline.py --out "Barca Reborn.csv"     # v6 by default
python evaluation/validate_submission.py "Barca Reborn.csv"
\end{verbatim}

The set-encoder weights are the only trained artefact in git
(\code{possession/models/encoder\_metrica\_s*.npz}, 3 $\times$ 1.8\,MB, fitted on IDSSE and Metrica
only). GPU retraining is not bit-for-bit repeatable, which is why we commit the weights. They can
be retrained with \code{possession/experiments/encoder\_train.py} (variant \code{metrica}) in a
separate torch environment. Inference is single-threaded float64, and the CSV is identical from run
to run: SHA-256\\
\code{a6ef47f6b3839a39b03909ceee2531bd7610820eb6943aa5774d8755a7d2d5ee}.\\
From a fresh clone, a fresh environment and the four tactical data files, the whole sequence above
reproduces that file in about 30 minutes on CPU.\\
All experiments and their measurements are logged in \code{BITACORA.md}.
